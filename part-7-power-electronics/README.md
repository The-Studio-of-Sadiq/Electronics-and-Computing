# Part VII — Power Electronics

Every electronic system draws its energy through a converter: a processor's 1.0 V rail, a phone charger, an electric car's 400 V inverter, a pump drive, a solar inverter. This part builds those converters. Its abstraction is the **switching converter**: semiconductor switches that are either fully on or fully off, alternating tens of thousands to millions of times a second, with inductors and capacitors that average the chopped waveform into the DC or AC voltage the load needs, at efficiencies of 95–99 %. Like every abstraction in the book, it holds only inside limits: the switching and conduction losses of real devices, the junction temperature those losses produce, the overshoot that stray inductance adds at every edge, the time a device survives a short circuit, and the frequency at which the averaged model stops describing what the circuit does. The four chapters move from the switch itself, through DC–DC and AC conversion, to the drives, chargers and grid converters in which power electronics meets machines and the power system.

## By the end of this part you should understand

- Why converters switch, how the commutation cell underlies every topology, and how Si MOSFETs, IGBTs, SiC MOSFETs and GaN HEMTs compare in on-state loss, switching energy, recovery charge and ruggedness.
- How switching and conduction losses are computed from datasheet values, how they set the junction temperature through steady and transient thermal impedances, and how the electrothermal loop is closed.
- How gates are driven and isolated, and how layout, snubbers, desaturation detection and soft turn-off protect fast switches.
- How the buck, boost and buck–boost converters set their conversion ratios, how their inductors and capacitors are sized for ripple and transients, what changes in discontinuous conduction, and where their losses go across the load range.
- What the averaged small-signal model is, why the boost has a right-half-plane zero, and how crossover and compensation are chosen.
- How rectifiers and PWM inverters convert between AC and DC, what harmonics they produce, and how their filters, ratings, protection and cooling are chosen.
- How these converters become motor drives, battery chargers, renewable-energy interfaces and grid-connected converters.

## How this part connects to the rest of the book

This part rests on Part I: the diode recovery and Schottky diodes of Chapter 2, the gate charge, ratings and electrothermal behaviour of power MOSFETs in Chapter 4, and the thyristors, IGBTs and wide-bandgap devices of Chapter 5. The error amplifiers and references of Chapters 8 and 10 sit inside converter controllers, and the PWM timers and real-time firmware of Chapters 17 and 25–28 generate the switching signals and close the loops.

Among the sibling books, *Engineering Physics* gives the one-section sketch of rectifiers and switching converters that this part extends ([Ch. 18 §18.8](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Engineering-Physics/blob/main/CHAPTERS/CHAPTER%2018.md)) and the thermal-resistance networks it uses ([Ch. 14 §14.7](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Engineering-Physics/blob/main/CHAPTERS/CHAPTER%2014.md)); *Materials Science and Engineering* explains why SiC and GaN outperform silicon ([Ch. 33](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Materials-Science-and-Engineering/blob/main/Chapters/CHAPTER%2033.md)). *Circuits, Signals and Communications* supplies the transient analysis, Fourier methods and the control theory (margins, loop shaping, digital control) on which converter design depends ([Ch. 34](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-7-control-system-fundamentals/34-feedback-and-stability.md), [Ch. 35](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-7-control-system-fundamentals/35-control-design-fundamentals.md)). *Electrical Energy and Machines* owns what lies beyond the converter's terminals: the machines and their drive control (V/f, FOC, DTC) in [Ch. 10–12](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Electrical-Energy-and-Machines/blob/main/part-3-motor-drives/12-ac-motor-drives.md), energy storage in [Ch. 16](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Electrical-Energy-and-Machines/blob/main/part-4-power-generation/16-energy-storage.md), and harmonic limits, grid codes and renewable grid integration in [Ch. 25](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Electrical-Energy-and-Machines/blob/main/part-7-power-quality-and-grid-modernization/25-power-quality.md) and [Ch. 27](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Electrical-Energy-and-Machines/blob/main/part-7-power-quality-and-grid-modernization/27-grid-integration-of-renewable-energy.md). This part stops at the converter hardware and its own loops.
